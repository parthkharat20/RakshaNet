RAKSHANET TECHNICAL DOSSIER SECTION 1: SYSTEM OVERVIEW & ARCHITECTURE

1. Executive Summary & Problem Formulation

1.1 The Operational Bottleneck: The "Golden Window"

In cyber-enabled financial fraud across India (e.g., UPI QR scams, Digital Arrest, Part-Time Job/Task fraud), stolen capital experiences rapid dissipation. Within 45 minutes of a victim being compromised, funds are partitioned across 3 to 4 sequential hops of synthetic, dormant, or rented "burner" mule accounts. Within 180 to 240 minutes (the Golden Window), foot runners physically withdraw untraceable currency notes from coordinated Automated Teller Machine (ATM) clusters in urban transit corridors.

The Fatal Flaw of Existing Bank Anti-Fraud Systems: Traditional bank fraud engines (FICO, Actimize, rule-based heuristics) operate transductively—evaluating historical behavioral anomalies on accounts with past transactions. However, mule syndicates recruit zero-history personas (students, rural gig workers) whose accounts appear pristine. By the time a zero-history account transacts suspiciously, the cash is already withdrawn. Law enforcement arrives 24 to 72 hours later to freeze empty ghost accounts.

1.2 The RakshaNet Architectural Paradigm Shift

RakshaNet replaces reactive post-mortem investigation with proactive topological and geospatial interdiction. Instead of asking "Did this account transact suspiciously in the past?", RakshaNet applies inductive Graph Neural Networks (GraphSAGE) to evaluate the structural position of zero-history accounts within the dynamic transaction graph. Concurrently, a geospatial engine combines HDBSCAN spatial density clustering with XGBoost classifiers on PostGIS coordinates to predict the physical ATM hotspots where liquidation will occur within the next 45 minutes.

2.3 Synthetic Fraud Benchmark Topology Modeling

Due to banking secrecy laws and the DPDP Act 2023, live transaction data cannot be exported. RakshaNet implements a calibrated synthetic transaction generator embedding three mathematical laundering topologies into background civilian traffic:

  • Star Dispersal (Fan-Out): 1 primary compromise node transfers funds across 15 to 25 spoke accounts in bursts below ₹50,000 to evade statutory AML transaction thresholds.
  • Multi-Hop Linear Chains: $A \to B \to C \to D$ (3 to 5 hops across different banks) with inter-hop delays of 3 to 8 minutes, defeating single-bank velocity heuristics.
  • Convergent Aggregation (Fan-In): 5 to 10 intermediary layering nodes pool funds into a single cash-out node immediately prior to physical withdrawal.
RAKSHANET TECHNICAL DOSSIER SECTION 3: MACHINE LEARNING & TOPOLOGICAL ALGORITHMS

3. Dual-Branch AI Fraud Prediction Pipeline

3.1 Branch 1: Topological Graph AI (Inductive GraphSAGE)

Mathematical Foundation: Unlike transductive graph algorithms (e.g., DeepWalk, Node2Vec) that require retraining the entire network when a new account appears, GraphSAGE learns localized aggregator functions that generalize inductively to previously unseen nodes.

Neighborhood Aggregation: h_{N(v)}^{(k)} = AGGREGATE_k ( { h_u^{(k-1)}, forall u in N(v) } )
Node Representation Update: h_v^{(k)} = sigma ( W^{(k)} * [ h_v^{(k-1)} || h_{N(v)}^{(k)} ] )

Where h_v^{(k)} is node v's embedding at layer k=2, N(v) is a uniformly sampled neighbor set, and W^{(k)} is a trainable projection matrix. A 2-layer mean aggregator is deployed to satisfy strict sub-50ms inference constraints.

Topological Link Prediction Head:

To evaluate if an unflagged account u is acting as a laundering link to suspect node v, learned embeddings are fused with graph-theoretic metrics:

Adamic-Adar Proximity: A(u, v) = sum_{w in N(u) cap N(v)} [ 1 / log |N(w)| ]
Link Probability: P(MuleHop_{u,v}) = sigma ( z_u^T * W_{pred} * z_v + beta * A(u, v) )

3.2 Branch 2: Geospatial ATM Cashout Forensics (HDBSCAN + XGBoost)

While fund transfers happen in milliseconds, physical ATM cashouts require foot travel and multiple withdrawals due to per-card daily withdrawal caps (₹20,000–₹50,000). RakshaNet predicts physical withdrawal locations using a two-stage spatial model:

  • Stage 1 (HDBSCAN Density Clustering): Clusters ATM locations and historical crime reports using mutual reachability distance based on PostGIS spherical geometry (`ST_DistanceSphere`):
    d_{mreach-k}(a, b) = max ( core_k(a), core_k(b), d_{geo}(a, b) )
  • Stage 2 (XGBoost Threat Scoring): Evaluates individual ATMs within identified clusters using an engineered spatial-temporal feature matrix:
    Feature Name Parameter Formulation Threat Hypothesis
    `dist_to_highway` d_{hwy} < 300m Proximity to major expressways enables rapid vehicular getaway across police borders.
    `booth_security_rating` Binary flag (0 = Guarded, 1 = Unmonitored) Isolated kiosks with single CCTV and no physical guard are heavily favored by cash runners.
    `nocturnal_hours` t in [23:00, 05:00] IST Syndicates exploit off-peak hours when branch staff and pedestrian footfall are minimal.
    `temporal_velocity_decay` V(t) = V_0 * e^{-lambda * t} Threat probability decays exponentially as elapsed time from initial debit increases.

3.3 Multi-Modal Risk Fusion & Explainable AI (SHAP)

The Composite Risk Score is calculated as: S_{fused} = 0.60 * S_{graph} + 0.40 * S_{geo}. To prevent black-box opacity in judicial proceedings, TreeSHAP decomposes the fused score into local feature attributions:

Shapley Value: phi_i(x) = sum_{S subseteq F \setminus {i}} [ |S|!(|F| - |S| - 1)! / |F|! ] * [ f(S union {i}) - f(S) ]

The system converts phi_i attributions into court-admissible legal text:

Court-Admissible Legal Explanation:
"Account #86174411141 scored 96.8% (Critical Threat): Topological bridge between two confirmed mule syndicates (+0.37 SHAP), High-risk ATM cluster proximity (+0.30 SHAP), Abnormal IFSC dispersal velocity (+0.15 SHAP)."
